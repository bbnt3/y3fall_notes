\header{Cash Matching Problem}
A series of future obligations must be met.
\\You have various bonds you can invest in, where the portfolio could be used to meet the obligation.
\\Choose portfolio of minimum cost that has enough money to meet the obligation (optimization!!)

Let $y_t$ be the obligation at time $t$, and $x_i$ the number of bond $i$ (price $p_i$, coupon $C_i$, face $F_i$) we buy.

Assumptions (the problem can be solved under each one):
\begin{enumerate}[label = \arabic*)]
    \item No reinvestment of excess cash is allowed.
    \item Excess cash can be held at zero interest from one period to the next.
    \item Excess cash can be reinvested at the appropriate forward rate.
\end{enumerate}

Constrained optimization?????
\\Yes: the objective (total cost) and every constraint are \dbb{linear} in the unknowns, so each model is a \rb{linear program} (LP), solvable with e.g.\ Excel Solver.

Note \# of bonds should always be non-negative! ($x_i \geq 0$, no short selling)

\heading{Model 1: Without Reinvestment}
Each period's cash flow from the bonds must cover that period's obligation on its own:
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}
    \min_{x_1,x_2,x_3} \quad & p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 + p_3x_3\\
    \text{s.t.} \quad & F_1x_1 + C_2x_2 + C_3x_3 \geq y_1\\
    & (F_2 + C_2)x_2 + C_3x_3 \geq y_2\\
    & (F_3 + C_3)x_3 \geq y_3\\
    & x_1, x_2, x_3 \geq 0
\end{aligned}}\]

\heading{Model 2: Reinvestment at Zero Rate}
$z_i$ is the possible excess cash generated, when positive, could be carried forward for reinvestment.
\\Excess $z_1$ is subtracted at time 1 and added back (with no interest) at time 2:
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}
    \min_{x_1,x_2,x_3} \quad & p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 + p_3x_3\\
    \text{s.t.} \quad & F_1x_1 + C_2x_2 + C_3x_3 - z_1 \geq y_1\\
    & (F_2 + C_2)x_2 + C_3x_3 + z_1 - z_2 \geq y_2\\
    & (F_3 + C_3)x_3 + z_2 \geq y_3\\
    & x_i \geq 0, \quad z_1, z_2 \geq 0
\end{aligned}}\]
Model 2 will never be worse than model one, worst case the same ($z=0$) than model 1, thus it is superior.

\heading{Model 3: Reinvestment at Forward Rate}
Model 3: investment with forward rate. Excess $z_1$ carried from time 1 to 2 now grows by $(1+f_{1,2})$:
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}
    \min_{x_1,x_2,x_3} \quad & p_1x_1 + p_2x_2 + p_3x_3\\
    \text{s.t.} \quad & F_1x_1 + C_2x_2 + C_3x_3 - z_1 \geq y_1\\
    & (F_2 + C_2)x_2 + C_3x_3 + (1+f_{1,2})z_1 - z_2 \geq y_2\\
    & (F_3 + C_3)x_3 + (1+f_{2,3})z_2 \geq y_3\\
    & x_i \geq 0, \quad z_1, z_2 \geq 0
\end{aligned}}\]
Similarly, as long as forward rates are non-negative, $(1+f)z \geq z$, so Model 3 is never worse than Model 2.

\begin{example}[Cash Matching Spreadsheet]
    Four bonds (face \$100, annual coupons), bond $i$ maturing at time $i$, and obligations $(10, 200, 30, 50)$ at times 1--4.
    \\The term structure uses \dbb{continuous compounding}: discount factors $d(0,i) = e^{-s_i i}$, and reinvesting from $i-1$ to $i$ multiplies by $e^{f_{i-1,i}}$ (e.g.\ $e^{0.04} = 1.0408$).
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{c|ccc|cccc|c}
        \toprule
        & \multicolumn{3}{c|}{Term structure} & \multicolumn{4}{c|}{Bond cash flows} & \\
        Time & $s_i$ & $f_{i-1,i}$ & $d(0,i)$ & 1 (3\%) & 2 (4\%) & 3 (2\%) & 4 (5\%) & Obligation\\
        \midrule
        1 & 3\% & 3.0\% & 0.9704 & 103 & 4 & 2 & 5 & 10\\
        2 & 3.5\% & 4.0\% & 0.9324 & & 104 & 2 & 5 & 200\\
        3 & 4\% & 5.0\% & 0.8869 & & & 102 & 5 & 30\\
        4 & 4.2\% & 4.8\% & 0.8454 & & & & 105 & 50\\
        \midrule
        \multicolumn{4}{r|}{Price} & 99.96 & 100.85 & 94.27 & 102.71 & \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    Each price is the bond's cash flows discounted at the spot rates, e.g.\ $p_1 = 103(0.9704) = 99.96$. Optimal portfolios:
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{lccccc}
        \toprule
        Model & $x_1$ & $x_2$ & $x_3$ & $x_4$ & Total cost\\
        \midrule
        1) No reinvestment & 0 & 1.8950 & 0.2708 & 0.4762 & \$265.546\\
        2) Zero rate ($z_1 = 0.484$) & 0 & 1.8903 & 0.2708 & 0.4762 & \$265.077\\
        3) Forward rate ($z = 21.25, 9.97, 9.19$) & 0.2104 & 1.7828 & 0.2626 & 0.3843 & \$265.059\\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item In Model 1, time 1 receives $\$10.50 > \$10$: the extra \$0.50 is wasted, since it can't be carried forward.
        \item In Model 2, that excess ($z_1 = 0.484$) is carried to time 2, so slightly fewer of bond 2 are needed.
        \item Cost decreases $1 \to 2 \to 3$, as expected.
        \item Model 3's cost equals the PV of the obligations at the spot rates: $10(0.9704) + 200(0.9324) + 30(0.8869) + 50(0.8454) = \$265.059$. With reinvestment at the forward rates, every dollar moves through time exactly as the term structure says, so no portfolio can do better than this.
    \end{itemize}
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Writing Out the Linear Programs]
    Same four bonds and obligations as above, with $x_j$ = units of bond $j$ and $z_t$ = cash carried from year $t$ to year $t+1$. All three models share the objective (prices from the term structure):
    \[\min \quad 99.955890x_1 + 100.850739x_2 + 94.271563x_3 + 102.710952x_4\]
    \b{1) No reinvestment:}
    \[\begin{aligned}
        103x_1 + 4x_2 + 2x_3 + 5x_4 &\geq 10\\
        104x_2 + 2x_3 + 5x_4 &\geq 200\\
        102x_3 + 5x_4 &\geq 30\\
        105x_4 &\geq 50\\
        x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4 &\geq 0
    \end{aligned}\]
    $x^* = (0, 1.894976, 0.270775, 0.476190)$, minimum cost \$265.546082. Year 1 has an excess of 0.502406 that is lost.

    \b{2) Carry cash at zero interest:} each $z_t$ leaves year $t$ and arrives unchanged in year $t+1$.
    \[\begin{aligned}
        103x_1 + 4x_2 + 2x_3 + 5x_4 - z_1 &\geq 10\\
        104x_2 + 2x_3 + 5x_4 + z_1 - z_2 &\geq 200\\
        102x_3 + 5x_4 + z_2 - z_3 &\geq 30\\
        105x_4 + z_3 &\geq 50\\
        x \geq 0, \quad z_1, z_2, z_3 &\geq 0
    \end{aligned}\]
    $x^* = (0, 1.890324, 0.270775, 0.476190)$, $z^* = (0.483798, 0, 0)$, minimum cost \$265.076934. Cash left after year 1 is available in year 2.

    \b{3) Reinvest at the forward rate:} each $z_t$ grows by the forward factor $e^{f_{t,t+1}}$ before arriving, i.e.\ $e^{0.04}$, $e^{0.05}$, $e^{0.048}$.
    \[\begin{aligned}
        103x_1 + 4x_2 + 2x_3 + 5x_4 - z_1 &\geq 10\\
        104x_2 + 2x_3 + 5x_4 + 1.040811z_1 - z_2 &\geq 200\\
        102x_3 + 5x_4 + 1.051271z_2 - z_3 &\geq 30\\
        105x_4 + 1.049171z_3 &\geq 50\\
        x \geq 0, \quad z_1, z_2, z_3 &\geq 0
    \end{aligned}\]
    Excel's portfolio, with minimum cost \$265.058524:
    \[x^* \approx (0.210372,\ 1.782777,\ 0.262646,\ 0.384342), \qquad z^* \approx (21.24643,\ 9.96928,\ 9.192063)\]

    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{lr}
        \toprule
        Model & Minimum initial cost\\
        \midrule
        No reinvestment & \$265.546082\\
        Zero interest carry & \$265.076934\\
        Forward rate carry & \$265.058524\\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    \ul{Allowing cash to move across years reduces the purchase cost.}
    \\At forward rates, \dbb{several portfolios} have the same minimum cost, so the optimal $x^*$ is not unique (Excel's answer is just one of them). This matches the note above: any portfolio that exactly meets the obligations costs the PV of the obligations.
\end{example}
